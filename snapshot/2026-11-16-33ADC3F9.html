"Who is Hiring?" postings stuck near 12-year lows as AI reaches half of all jobs (November 2026)

来源: Hacker News Hiring Trends 快照日期: 2026-11-16

November Highlights

Saturday, November 14, 2026

It's been two and a half years since the last update, so this one covers a lot of ground. As of November 14, November's thread has 244 job postings, down 37% from 387 at the same point last year. Every thread since July has landed between 224 and 255 postings, and August's 224 was the fewest since January 2014. So far 2026 averages 287 postings a month, the lowest yearly average since 2013.

At the same time, AI is now mentioned in more than half of all postings (51.6%), up from 30% two years ago.

AI is in half of all postings

AI appears in 51.6% of November's postings, second only to Remote. It crossed 50% for the first time in June 2026. Two years ago it was 30%.

The work itself is shifting toward agents. AI agents (including "agentic") appear in 14.8% of postings, ahead of LLMs at 10.7%. MCP barely existed a year ago (0.3%) and is now in 4.5% of postings, the biggest climb in this month's rankings. Meanwhile, the older machine learning label slipped from 14.8% to 11.5%.

Front-end roles keep shrinking

Figures in this section are three-month averages, which smooth out month-to-month swings, so they differ a little from the November-only numbers in the chart and tables. Front End has had the steepest fall of any role label: from 27% of postings in early 2022 to 6.6% now, and from 15.6% a year ago. Full Stack (26.2% to 20.1%) and Back End (23.6% to 18.8%) also dropped over the past year, most of it in the last few months.

Seniority mix has been steadier. Senior has stayed around 30% for years, Staff has roughly quadrupled since 2019 to about 12%, and Junior remains rare at around 3%.

Changes to the data

New terms this month: AI Agents, LLM, MCP, RAG, LangChain, Anthropic, pgvector, Vector Database, Bun and Deno. Every thread back to 2011 is re-analyzed on each run, so their full history is included.

I also fixed a bug where a few terms with alternate spellings didn't count their own name (for example, "Next.js" was only counted when written "NextJS"). Next.js, NestJS, WebAssembly, Front End and Back End are higher across all months than in earlier posts. The December 2011 thread, which has a single posting, is no longer included.
